* Day 1 <2026-09-09 Wed>

** Arfon Smith - [[https://zenodo.org/records/22672891][Research Software and the future of trustworthy science : Where does the rigour go?]]

+ Runs a journal that is perfect target for AI submissions but also a funder.
+ See [[https://aicoding.leaflet.pub/3mbrvhyye4k2e][/Relocating Rigour/]] at https://aicoding.leaflet.pub/ talks about mistakes in Extreme Programming was missing
  up-front specification and mistake to think there was no rigour. It had actually moved, from being up-front to showing
  it to those you are delivering the product to.
+ Fast science is good but unverified science is not.
+ Generative AI is here to stay even if model progress stopped the world has already changed permanently.
+ Not mourning a loss but worried, mixed emotions grief, enthusiasm, anger, denial and exhaustion. (I'd add in
  scepticism).
+ KPI of research software is research.

*** High Level Summary

+ AI capability progress is real.
+ Models can work for a number of hours and produce software that is 50-80% "effective"/"functional".
+ Same for image classification and other metrics (Stanford Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence).
+ Open-models are now very effective too.
+ [[https://aria.org.uk/insights/can-an-ai-scientist-really-discover-knowledge][Can an AI Scientist really discover knowledge?]]
+ Claude Code moment - agentic coding threshold became useful
+ Agentic coding moved from repair to creation (higher order activities)
+ Implication for RSE is that PI/researchers can solve their problems with significantly less support.
+ https://anthropic.com/research/economic-index-primitives
+ Communities are being disrupted. The Missing GitHub Status Page shows availability is decreasing.
+ Can disable pull requests on repositories to block slop.
+ Low quality bug submissions
+ [[https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-2026][The impact of AI on software engineers in 2026: key trends. Part 1]]
+ [[https://blog.lmorchard.com/2026/03/11/grief-and-the-ai-split/][Grief and the AI Split - blog.lmorchard.com]] - there are two types of people writing software, those who do it for the
  craft and those who do it for the results, the former are mourning a loss, the later less so as they are results
  focused.

+ Question : What happens when the capacity to produce software grows much faster than the capacity to review and absorb
  it?

+ [[https://openai.com/index/paperbench/][PaperBench: Evaluating AI’s Ability to Replicate AI Research | OpenAI]]
+ [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24591][[2510.24591] ReplicationBench: Can AI Agents Replicate Astrophysics Research Papers?]]

+ Terminal-bench science
+ Genebench pro

+ Andrej Karpathy - Software 1.0 automates what you can specify, Software 2.0 automates what you can verify (through
  reinforcement learning)
+ Physical science requires real world - /you can't unit-test a wet lab result!/
+ Model Hardware Standard - operate laboratory instruments safely.
+ Software progress is rapid because it is easily verifiable.

+ [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10181][[2602.10181] Why do we do astrophysics?]] - we do science because its interesting to know stuff, AI can do a lot of this
  work, but automating it doesn't accelerate the discipline rather it would remove the point of doing it.
+ Much of science remains expensive to verify, which parts of science we make cheaper to verify and why is a choice.

*** Part 2 - What does this mean?

+ Production decoupled from verification. More commits > more completed projects > more releases.
+ Writing code is cheaper, shipping, verifying and maintaining it has not
+ Rigour has moved elsewhere friction
+ "AI is an accelerant, not the kindling. The kindling is publish or perish" - Ivan Oransky, Retraction Watch
+ We use vanity metrics that reward volume over rigour, not surprising that we are seeing bad outcomes.
+ "We have allowed execution to be a substitute for thought" . [[https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02837-2][Large language models are not the problem 2026 Hiranya
  Peiris]] ([[https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22071][[2604.22071] Large language models are not the problem]])
+ JOSS was using poor metrics that could easily be attained by AI.
+ Peer review process is in big trouble.
+ Can make something but is it useful.
+ Google Deepmind - validation bottleneck
+ PLOS checkability
+ Trust, validation and traceability are really important
+ Reproducibility is getting cheaper to implement (version control, environment, tests, docs and packaged workflow). Can
  be cheap and vacuous though.
+ Raise the bar from evidence of good practice to evidence of reproducibility of results (use agents etc. to undertake
  reproducibile testing).
+ When production scales, reproducibility becomes _essential for progress_.
